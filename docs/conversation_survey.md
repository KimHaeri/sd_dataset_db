# Conversational speech survey (2026-11-03)

Candidates for diarization data under the scope widened on 2026-11-03: any real multi-speaker
conversation whose speaker turns can be obtained, either from utterance or segment timestamps
with speaker labels (**A**) or by VAD on a close-talk or separate channel per speaker (**B**).

**These are survey leads, not DB facts.** Four searches (speech conferences and challenges;
LREC, *CL and corpus archives such as TalkBank, BAS and CLARIN; GitHub, Zenodo and OpenSLR;
Hugging Face) opened each dataset's page and copied the licence wording, access, size and
annotation as found there. A "?" means the page did not confirm it. Nothing here has gone
through the DB's evidence rules yet, except the datasets listed below, which were checked
and added to `data/datasets.csv`. Add the others one at a time with the manual's
"Workflow: add a dataset".

Grouping follows the licence as found, under the current tier rules (`docs/manual.md`,
"License tiers"); the tier itself is decided per dataset when it is added. Datasets already in
the DB, and plain mirrors of them, are left out. 162 datasets in total.

Checked and added to the DB on 2026-11-03: `indic_diarbench` (CC BY 4.0, test splits only:
eval), `nb_samtale` (CC0: train), `hcrc_maptask` (CC BY 4.0: train), `primock57` (CC BY 4.0:
train), `otospeech_fd_280h` (CC BY 4.0 in the card metadata, gated: train).

Things to know before using a group:

- **LDC, ELRA and paid releases** are the largest source of type B data (two-channel telephone
  calls with transcripts), but each needs a licence purchase.
- **TalkBank CABank** corpora are free after accepting the TalkBank ground rules (CC BY-NC-SA
  terms), so they are evaluation candidates; whether each CHAT file carries time bullets has to
  be checked per corpus.
- **Hugging Face re-uploads** often carry a licence tag that differs from the original release;
  the original's terms apply.
- **Data cut from online videos** (MISP-M3SD, parts of Indic DiarBench, VoxMM) carries a
  licence for the annotations; the rights of the videos themselves are usually not stated.
- **Commercial vendors** publish small samples under open tags; the full sets are paid.
